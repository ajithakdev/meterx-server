MeterX is a free, open-source browser extension that tests download, upload, ping, jitter and bufferbloat from your toolbar. No account, nothing tracked.

The quick test takes about 5 seconds and uses up to 25 MB.

What can Mbps do?

    Counts use the extension's own rules: 25 Mbps for each 4K stream and 5 Mbps for HD. Calls and gaming depend on upload and ping too, which MeterX measures.

    More than one number

    Speed under load, not just at rest

    A line can look fast and still stutter on a call while something downloads. MeterX pings during the download and upload, shows how far the delay climbs, and grades it from A to F.

    Every test, kept on your device

    The side panel keeps up to 200 results with a trend chart, so a slow evening stands out from a slow line. Run tests on a timer and get a notice when quality drops.

    Against the plan you pay for

    Enter the speeds your provider advertises and see what share of them actually arrives, for download and upload, over any range.

    A report you can hand to your provider

    Export a PDF with averages, ranges, a trend chart and every result, or CSV and JSON with units in every column. Each report carries an ID that changes if any figure is edited.

    Private, open and yours

    No account and no analytics. Results stay in your browser. The test server keeps no logs, the code is open under AGPL-3.0, and you can point MeterX at your own server instead.

    Connect

    MeterX opens a connection to the nearest Cloudflare location, or to your own server. Under a second.

    Ping and jitter

    A run of small pings measures the delay and how much it wobbles from one ping to the next.

    Download

    Four parallel streams fill your line for a fixed time. The slow start and the tail are trimmed, so the figure is the steady speed.

    Upload

    Three streams do the same upward, while pings keep running to catch the delay that builds under load.

    Questions, answered

    Is it free?

    Yes, with no paid tier, no account and no ads. The code is open source under AGPL-3.0.

    What leaves my browser?

    Only the test traffic itself: bytes downloaded from and uploaded to the test server, and the pings. Results are stored in your browser and nowhere else. The server keeps no logs. The privacy page has the details.

    Why is my result different from other speed tests?

    Each test uses its own servers, number of streams and length. MeterX tests against the nearest Cloudflare location with several streams for a fixed time and trims the slow start, which is close to how your browser loads real pages and downloads.

    What is bufferbloat?

    When a line is busy, routers can queue too much data, so every ping waits in line. Speed looks fine but calls and games lag. MeterX measures ping while the line is full and grades how much it rises.

    Can I use my own test server?

    Yes. The repository includes a small server you can run on your own machine or network, and the settings page lets you point MeterX at it.

    When is it coming to Chrome?

    The Chrome Web Store listing is planned. Until then MeterX is available for Firefox, Edge and Opera.
